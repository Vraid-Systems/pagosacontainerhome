Mountain biking

Local trails usually ride from late April into November. The high-country rides open up once the snow melts out, roughly midsummer. When the mountains are snowed in, head south to the desert.

Before you ride
  • The October 2025 floods damaged trails and roads around Pagosa, and some trails still haven't been assessed. Check Trailforks and the Forest Service conditions page.
  • E-bikes aren't allowed on non-motorized national forest trails.
  • Most of these rides have no cell service. Download the maps before you go.

Trail details

Also good

  • Turkey Springs trail network. The main local system: 30-plus miles of beginner-to-intermediate singletrack and old two-track up Piedra Road. Signage can be confusing, so carry a map. Cattle graze here; close the gates behind you. Pagosa Area Trails Council
  • Reservoir Hill, downtown. A few miles of in-town singletrack with flow and downhill lines, plus mellow loops. It usually dries out by mid-April. Trail info
  • Cloman Park, Piedra Road. A short beginner and intermediate figure-eight. Good for kids or a quick spin. Trail info
  • Bike shop: Pagosa Mountain Sports, downtown, for sales, service and rentals (including e-bikes).

Dirt bikes and OHVs

  • Download the free Motor Vehicle Use Map for the Pagosa Ranger District. It shows which roads and trails are open to what, and when. Most motorized trail seasons end November 30.
  • Riding from out of state? You need a Colorado OHV permit for designated OHV trails and roads on public land, plated dirt bikes included. Colorado Parks and Wildlife

Trail running

Windy Pass is the close, lower option. The rest are high-country runs between 11,000 and 13,000 feet. They're snow-free in summer and early fall. Afternoon thunderstorms are routine in summer, so start early, carry a layer, and turn around if the sky builds.

Windy Pass HOST PICK

4.3 mi each way · 1,974 ft · tops out at 9,947 ft

Hike up, jog down. The trail starts right off US-160 on the way to Wolf Creek, across from the West Fork Road turnoff, and climbs through forest and meadows to big views. Park at the National Forest sign just past West Fork Road and take care crossing the highway. Expect some downed trees, and snow up high into late spring.

Summit Peak, The Unicorn & Montezuma Peak HOST PICK

8.3 mi loop · 3,361 ft · tops out at 13,299 ft

Summit Peak is the high point of the South San Juans. The loop crosses three 13,000-foot peaks on Class 2 off-trail ridges with some route-finding, so it's for experienced mountain runners. Getting there: take Park Creek Road (FR 380), which leaves US-160 on the South Fork side of Wolf Creek Pass. It's a long dirt road, so go slow and bring good tires. Skip Pass Creek Road unless you have a serious off-road rig.

Hot springs

Pagosa comes from a Ute word usually translated as "healing waters," and the town sits on top of them. Two free soaks we love, and three resorts for when you want towels and a changing room.

Nathan's Hippy Dip HOST PICK

Free · in town

Rock-walled pools at the edge of the San Juan River, so they change with the water level. Swimsuits on, no pets in the pools, and pack out your trash.

Map

Piedra River Hot Springs HOST PICK

Free · about 2.8 mi round trip · steep climb out

Primitive pools by the Piedra River. Take US-160 about 20 miles west of downtown, then First Fork Road (FS 622) about 7 miles north to the Sheep Creek Trailhead. The pools farthest from the river are the hottest. Pack out everything.

Directions and details

Piedra: call before you drive out The October 2025 floods damaged some Forest Service roads, and First Fork Road is a long dirt road with blind corners. Check it's open with the Pagosa Ranger District, (970) 264-2268.

The resorts

  • The Springs Resort & Spa, downtown on the river. The big one, with dozens of pools, adults-only areas and day passes sold online. It sits on the Mother Spring, which Guinness World Records recognized as the deepest geothermal hot spring after a plumb line reached 1,002 feet without hitting bottom.
  • Overlook Hot Springs Spa, downtown in a historic building. Rooftop tubs over the river, indoor tubs with a cold plunge, and a private tub room. No reservation needed to soak.
  • Healing Waters Resort & Spa, in town. A big outdoor mineral pool, a hot tub, and separate indoor men's and women's hot baths. No reservations. The pools close about every other Wednesday, so check their calendar.
  • No dogs at any of the resorts.
  • In natural hot springs, keep your head above water. Why (CDC)
  • Rainbow Hot Springs is out of reach: the floods destroyed the West Fork trail bridges, and the Forest Service doesn't expect replacements before 2027.

About the house

  • Heading east over Wolf Creek Pass? Leave Pagosa with a high charge. The ski area's one slow charger has been unreliable, South Fork has Level 2 only, and the next dependable fast chargers are in Del Norte. The climb to the pass is about 3,700 feet, and cold weather costs range too.
  • Heading west? Durango has a Tesla Supercharger and a Rivian Adventure Network site, both open to all EVs.
  • Also uptown: Walmart, Mountain Spirits Liquor & Wine, Terry's Ace Hardware, and gas at City Market and the Shell on North Pagosa Boulevard. Natural Grocers is at the east end of downtown.

Winter

Wolf Creek is known for the most snow in Colorado, and it's just up the road.

Wolf Creek Ski Area

  • 23 miles east of Pagosa Springs on US-160, at the top of Wolf Creek Pass. Family-owned, no-frills, and known for about 430 inches of snow a year. Getting there
  • Opening day depends on natural snow, and some years it's in October or November. Watch the snow report.
  • Skinning up: last season, uphill traffic was allowed on Kelly Boyce to Divide Trail during lift hours with an uphill pass from the ticket office. Early in the season they steered skinners to Lobo Overlook instead. Check the current snow report before you go.

Splitboarding and backcountry

Even "low consequence" is avalanche terrain
  • Read the CAIC avalanche forecast for the South San Juan zone the morning you go.
  • Carry a beacon, shovel and probe, know how to use them, and don't go alone.
  • CDOT closes the pass for avalanche work after big storms. Have a plan B.

Driving the pass

  • The dirt road up to the house is fine in a regular car most of the year. November through April, bring AWD or good snow tires. Wolf Creek Pass is the real test: it tops out at 10,857 feet with long 7% grades, so check conditions before you drive in from the east.
  • When Colorado's traction law is on, you need snow-rated tires (M+S or the mountain-snowflake symbol) with at least 3/16-inch tread, or chains or an approved traction device. Under the chain law, every vehicle needs chains or a device. Many rental cars and EVs come on worn all-seasons, so check your tread.
  • Check COtrip for closures and cameras before you head up.

Nordic skiing and snowshoeing

  • Cloman Park, just off Piedra Road, is the closest groomed skiing to the house, with easier loops. No dogs on the ranch trails.
  • Coyote Hill, about 6 miles up Piedra Road, has more terrain: up to 14 km, beginner to advanced, plus a snowshoe and fat-bike loop. Dogs are OK if they're leashed or under control.
  • The Pagosa Nordic Club grooms them for free (donations welcome). Here's the grooming report.

Dogs

  • In town: dogs are welcome in town parks and on the riverwalk on a leash, with bag stations along the way. Town parks
  • National forest: keep dogs leashed at trailheads, campgrounds and picnic areas. On the trails, keep them under control and close by. Cattle graze on many trails, so keep dogs away from them and close gates behind you.
  • Good dog walks: Treasure Falls on the way up Wolf Creek Pass (a short, steep walk to a 105-foot waterfall, leash on), Reservoir Hill in town (town park, leash on), and Coyote Hill on Piedra Road.
  • Not for dogs: the hot springs resorts. Chimney Rock doesn't allow dogs on its trails, but it has free shaded kennels at the visitor center.
  • Wildlife: mountain lions have taken dogs around Pagosa, and bears are active. Don't leave dogs outside alone, especially at dawn and dusk.

Vets

  • Elk Park Animal Hospital, on US-160 west of town, closest to the house: (970) 731-6400.
  • San Juan Veterinary Hospital, Pagosa Springs.
  • After hours: AspenTree Animal Care in Durango lists a 24-hour emergency line, (970) 863-8269. Call before you drive.

Off days

For rain, wind, sore legs, or a closed pass.

  • Soak. Overlook has indoor tubs, and Healing Waters has indoor hot baths. A cold, gray day is the best day for it.
  • Thingamajig Theatre at the Pagosa Springs Center for the Arts runs professional shows through fall and winter.
  • Treasure Falls, about 15 miles east of town on US-160: a short, steep walk to a 105-foot waterfall. Icy in winter.
  • Chimney Rock National Monument: ancestral Puebloan great houses with guided tours, roughly mid-May to mid-October (closed in winter). It's also a certified DarkSky Park.
  • Pagosa Springs History Museum: area history, open in summer.
  • Riff Raff Brewing for a long lunch by the river.
